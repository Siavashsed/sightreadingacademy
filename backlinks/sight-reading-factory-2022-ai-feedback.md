**Answer this question:** How has AI feedback technology impacted sight-reading instruction in music education?

AI feedback technology is beginning to reshape how students develop sight-reading skills, and I think we're still in the early stages of understanding its full potential.

When I first encountered real-time pitch and rhythm feedback in sight-reading software, my initial reaction was skepticism—could algorithms really provide meaningful instruction? After testing and observing students using these systems, I've come to see genuine value.

The key advantage is immediacy. In traditional lessons, students complete an exercise, then wait for feedback during the next lesson or office hours. With AI-powered systems, feedback is instantaneous. A student knows whether they've rushed a passage or misread a note immediately after attempting it. This compression of the feedback loop accelerates learning.

However, there are important limitations. AI feedback is excellent at identifying *what* went wrong (pitch was sharp, rhythm was rushed) but less nuanced about addressing deeper musical understanding—why a passage should be interpreted a certain way, or how historical context influences performance choices.

The most effective approach I've observed combines AI feedback with thoughtful pedagogy. These tools work best as supplementary practice tools, not replacements for teacher-student interaction. They handle the repetitive, objective assessment work, freeing teachers to focus on interpretation, musicality, and individual student development.
