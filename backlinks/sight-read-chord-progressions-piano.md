## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What is the best way to improve at sight-reading piano chord progressions quickly?

The most effective approach is to stop sight-reading chords as individual notes and start recognizing them as harmonic patterns. Here's why this matters and how to implement it:

Most pianists read chords the way they read melodies—note by note, hand by hand. This creates a fundamental speed limit. Your eyes and hands are constantly catching up to each other, and you're making micro-decisions about finger placement instead of thinking musically.

Instead, train your brain to recognize chord shapes and their functions instantly. When you see a G7 chord, you should recognize it as a dominant function seeking resolution, not as three individual notes to identify and play separately.

The practical method involves three components:

First, build a mental library of chord voicings in common keys. Not every voicing, but the most frequent ones you'll encounter in real music.

Second, understand harmonic function—knowing that IV moves to V, that vi can substitute for I, etc. This predictability means you're anticipating what comes next rather than reacting to what you see.

Third, practice strategic hand positioning. Your hands should move as one unit toward harmonic centers, not bounce independently between notes.

I've documented a comprehensive method that combines these elements with specific exercises in a detailed breakdown here, drawing from conservatory training approaches that actually translate to faster, more confident reading. The difference in student progress has been remarkable—we're talking weeks, not months, to see significant improvement.
