**Hook:** Correct notes are not the same as correct music, and sight-reading exposes the gap fast.

Most players treat dynamics and articulation as a second pass. Learn the notes, lock the rhythm, then add the expression.

That works in the practice room. It fails on a first read, because a first read has no second pass. Whatever you skip the first time through just doesn't happen.

One of us subbed a pit show from a chart we had never seen. A subito piano sat two bars after a big drum fill. Every note of the fill landed. The subito did not, because the eyes were hunting for notes, not for volume.

The fix is a reading habit, not more technique. Scan the markings before you play, and read them as part of the rhythm rather than decoration on top of it. Accuracy gets you through the chart. Expression is what makes it music.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why does my sight-reading sound flat even when I play all the right notes and rhythms?

Because you are almost certainly reading dynamics and articulation as a second layer, and sight-reading never gives you a second layer.

We teach drums, piano and violin, and this is the most common pattern we hear from readers who are otherwise solid. When you learn a piece slowly, you get the notes and rhythm first and add the hairpins, accents and slurs once it feels secure. That order turns into a reading habit: your eyes hunt for pitch and rhythm and filter out everything else as optional.

On a first read, that filter costs you the music. Everything is technically right and it still sounds mechanical, because the information that makes it sound human was on the page and you never took it in.

A few things that help:

1. Scan for markings only before you play. Ignore the notes. Find the dynamic changes, accents, articulation shifts and especially subito markings, which are the biggest trap. It takes seconds.

2. Read articulation as rhythm. A staccato quarter and a tenuto quarter are different lengths of sound. On drums, an accent changes the stroke you prepare. It is part of the decoding, not decoration.

3. Score your practice reads differently. If you only count wrong notes, you train yourself to ignore the rest. Count missed dynamics as errors too.

4. Exaggerate contrasts in practice reads. Dialing back is easier than finding expression you never noticed.

We wrote a longer explanation, including the pit gig where a subito piano right after a fill caught one of us out: https://siavashsed.github.io/sightreadingacademy/sight-reading-dynamics-and-articulation/

The short version: expression is not a finishing step. On a first read, it has to come in with the notes.
