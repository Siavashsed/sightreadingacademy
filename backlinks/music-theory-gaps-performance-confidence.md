## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do music students perform well in lessons but freeze during recitals and performances?

This is one of the most common challenges I see across all age groups, and while performance anxiety certainly plays a role, I've found that the real culprit is often hiding beneath the surface.

In my 20 years of teaching—both at Juilliard and in community settings—I've noticed that students who crash mid-performance almost always share one thing: unidentified theory gaps. They're not playing from understanding; they're playing from muscle memory alone.

Consider what happens under pressure: adrenaline floods your system, and your conscious mind goes quiet. When that happens, what carries you through? Only genuine understanding. If a student can't articulate why a harmonic progression works, or explain the structural purpose of a passage, their fingers will betray them the moment stress hits.

I worked with a talented 14-year-old who played a Clementi sonatina flawlessly in lessons but would freeze during recitals. When I investigated, he couldn't explain the key changes or harmonic function of certain sections. We spent two weeks filling those gaps—nothing fancy, just targeted theory work. His next performance was transformed. He wasn't fighting his memory anymore; he was playing from understanding.

The fix isn't more practice or therapy for nerves. It's identifying those specific theory gaps and building a solid mental map of the piece. Once students truly understand what they're playing, performance confidence follows naturally.

If you're experiencing this, consider having a candid conversation with your teacher about the harmonic and structural elements you're less clear on. Often, ten minutes of clarity beats ten hours of nervous repetition.
